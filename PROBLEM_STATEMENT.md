# From Basic Perception to Contextual Mathematical Meaning

Basic visual perception requires a model to read numbers, symbols, shapes and markers in one mathematical image. Advanced visual perception additionally requires interpreting their mathematical function in context.

Similar visual forms can retain meaning across images: π/4 radians and 45° can label equal angles, and two square angle markers can both denote perpendicularity. They can also change meaning: π/4 may be an angle or an x-coordinate; a square outline may be an angle marker or a grid cell; x may denote an angle or a length.

This benchmark asks whether a model can locate one specified shared or similar target in two images, explain its mathematical meaning in each, and judge whether those meanings match. It separates reading a form from understanding its contextual use.

Whether current models consistently possess this ability is an empirical question. The pilot does not generalize from a few failures to all models or assume basic perception is solved. Different-type pairs are the principal setting, with same-type controls. This release provides data and a protocol without model-performance results.

Only one target per pair is annotated. Other similarities and differences are outside the label. It contains the visual target, both mathematical meanings, a yes/no semantic judgment and its rationale in separate fields. Visual similarity does not imply semantic equivalence, while visually different expressions may be numerically equivalent.
